Periodic Table Quiz

How well do you know the elements? Find them on the periodic table, match names, symbols, and atomic numbers, or see how many you can get in 60 seconds. Start with the first 20 and work up to all 118; the elements you miss come back for review.

How to play. Pick a mode and a level. In Find by name, click or tap the element on the table; in Blank table, the boxes are empty, so you need to know where it sits. In the name, symbol, and number modes, pick one of four answers or press 1–4. A round is 10 questions; the sprint is as many as you can answer in 60 seconds, and Pause or P stops its clock. Press Enter for the next question. With a keyboard, the arrow keys move around the table, typing a symbol’s letters jumps to it (in Find by name), and Enter or Space chooses. The square button next to New round shows the quiz full screen.

Review my misses. Every element you miss, in any mode, joins your review list until you get it right twice in a row. Review rounds mix all the question types and draw the elements you miss most often more often. Your table, below, shows where you stand on all 118. Your best streak in each mode and level, your best sprint at each level, and your record are saved in this browser.

Names and symbols follow IUPAC, including the four names approved on 28 November 2016: nihonium (Nh, 113), moscovium (Mc, 115), tennessine (Ts, 117), and oganesson (Og, 118) (IUPAC announcement). The quiz uses the US spellings aluminum and cesium; IUPAC writes aluminium and caesium. The layout, groups, and periods follow IUPAC’s periodic table (version of 4 May 2022), with the lanthanoids (La–Lu) and actinoids (Ac–Lr) in their own rows. IUPAC has not settled whether La and Ac or Lu and Lr belong in group 3, so the quiz gives those 30 elements no group number.
