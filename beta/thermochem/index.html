Thermochemistry Lab BETA

Bond energies, reaction enthalpies and photodissociation thresholds from Argonne's Active Thermochemical Tables (ATcT). Type a species or a reaction; the tool balances it, converts units, finds every fragmentation channel up to a photon or electron energy, including products in excited electronic states, and draws an energy diagram.

Key terms
ΔfH°
Enthalpy of formation: the energy to make one mole of a species from its elements in their standard states.
D₀ and D₂₉₈
Bond dissociation energy at 0 K (from the lowest quantum state) and bond dissociation enthalpy at 298.15 K.
Threshold
The longest wavelength whose photon carries enough energy to break the bond, assuming cold molecules and no barrier.
IE and AE
Ionization energy (molecule to its cation) and appearance energy (molecule to a fragment ion, a neutral and an electron).
Uncertainty
ATcT quotes 95 % limits. Their values are correlated, so a simple sum of squares can be too large or too small; ATcT's covariances fix that.
Excited products
Fragments formed in an electronically excited state, such as O(¹D) or OH(A); the threshold rises by the excitation energy.
DEA
Dissociative electron attachment: a molecule captures a free electron and falls apart into a negative ion and a neutral.
Thermochemical Network
ATcT solves thousands of measurements and calculations together; each release has a version number (here TN 1.228).

Beta, in review. The Self-test tab recomputes bond, ionization, excited-product and electron-attachment energies and compares them with reference values, mostly the measurements ATcT itself uses; treat other results as provisional. Found a wrong number? Email foley.photon@gmail.com.

      How to cite

      ATcT's table attributes its values to the two method papers [1, 2] and to the network version used [3]:

        Cite this tool only for the arithmetic (balancing, unit conversion, channel search); the thermochemistry is ATcT's.

        Covariances

        Correlated uncertainties use the ATcT covariance matrix of ΔfH°(298.15 K) for 1874 gas-phase species, fetched from the ATcT API (/api/v1/covariance/species/) on 2026-11-06. The API serves TN 1.220, not 1.228. The page keeps only the pairs that change the variance of a sum or difference of two species by at least 0.1 % (326 497 of 1 732 591), which changes two-body fragmentation uncertainties by 0.04 % (median) and 0.24 % (99th percentile). Each correlation coefficient is stored with enough digits that its rounding changes no pair combination by more than 0.5 %, including strongly correlated pairs such as Br₂ and Br, where 1 − r ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶ sets the uncertainty of D₀(Br₂). The tool uses the TN 1.220 covariances as served, except that a species whose printed uncertainty changed between TN 1.220 and TN 1.228 has its row and column scaled to the TN 1.228 uncertainty; both values are shown. The file (2.1 MB, about 0.4 MB compressed) is loaded only when a reaction, channel list, diagram or the self-test needs it.

Where ATcT lists a state (14 cases, such as O(¹D), O₂(a¹Δg), CO(a³Π), ¹CH₂ and the halogen ²P½ levels), the ATcT entry is used instead, so no state is counted twice. States given only as estimates (SO a¹Δ, BrO and IO X₂) and species without an ATcT ground state (P, NS) are left out.

        T₀ for molecules is the Huber–Herzberg (0,0) band origin to the ground state where one is listed, otherwise chained through a lower state or computed as Tₑ + G′(0) − G″(0). Huber and Herzberg give some origins from the zero point of the Hill–Van Vleck expression rather than from the lowest level (their notes on OH, SH, CH and CN); these are converted to lowest-level T₀ with the spin-orbit and rotational constants on the same pages (OH A²Σ⁺ +38.18 cm⁻¹, SH A²Σ⁺ +170.23 cm⁻¹, CH A²Δ +28.27 cm⁻¹, CN A²Π −23.06 cm⁻¹). Where the value refers to a multiplet centre or to a hypothetical J = 0 level instead, it is not converted; the spread of the multiplet (|A|ΛS, up to 146 cm⁻¹ for O₂ A′³Δu) or the offset (about 1.3 cm⁻¹ for NO) is added to the uncertainty as an allowance. Rows marked “note” say which. Huber and Herzberg stop at 1977.

        Use your own table

        Load a CSV with the columns of the cleaned ATcT table (atct_id, formula, state, charge, composition, dHf_0K_kJmol, dHf_298K_kJmol, unc_95_kJmol…), for example a newer ATcT version. It stays in your browser. atct_id, formula and dHf_298K_kJmol are required. Composition and charge are read from their columns, or parsed from the formula where those are missing; rows where neither works are skipped and counted, so the balance check never runs without a composition. The ATcT covariances and the version diff are not applied to a CSV; the excited states are, by ATcT ID.

        How it is calculated

        Every result is a sum of ATcT enthalpies of formation, ΔrH° = Σ νi ΔfH°i, with ν positive for products. The tool checks that elements and charge balance before it shows a number; electrons (e-) carry ΔfH° = 0.

        • Bond dissociation energy D₀(A–B) = ΔfH°₀(A) + ΔfH°₀(B) − ΔfH°₀(AB); D₂₉₈ uses the 298.15 K values.
        • Photodissociation threshold λ = NAhc / D₀ = 119 626.6 nm·kJ mol⁻¹ / D₀. It is the one-photon limit for a molecule in its lowest rovibrational level with no barrier above the products. Parent internal energy lowers the real onset; a barrier (common for molecular channels such as H₂CO → H₂ + CO) raises it.
        • Available energy at wavelength λ is Ehν − D₀, shared among product translation, rotation, vibration and electronic excitation.
        • Ionization and appearance energies are 0 K differences: IE = ΔfH°₀(M⁺) − ΔfH°₀(M) and AE(A⁺) = ΔfH°₀(A⁺) + ΔfH°₀(B) − ΔfH°₀(AB). ATcT's 298.15 K ion values follow the stationary-electron convention, in which the electron carries no enthalpy at any temperature; to treat free electrons as a thermal gas, add 5/2 RT = 6.197 kJ/mol per electron produced at 298.15 K.
        • Proton affinity PA(M) = ΔfH°₂₉₈(M) + ΔfH°₂₉₈(H⁺) − ΔfH°₂₉₈(MH⁺).
        • Excited products. A fragment in an excited electronic state X* has ΔfH°₀(X*) = ΔfH°₀(X) + T₀(X*), with T₀ the term value above the lowest level from NIST (atoms: ASD; diatomics: Huber and Herzberg; polyatomics: Jacox). Where ATcT lists the state itself its own value is used. A channel's excited thresholds are D₀ plus the sum of the products' T₀; no 298.15 K values are given for these. The uncertainty of such a state adds the term value's own uncertainty, where the source gives one (Jacox gives ±0.05 to ±10 cm⁻¹), and an allowance where the source refers the state to a level other than the lowest, to the ATcT uncertainty of the ground state.
        • Electron channels. Dissociative electron attachment AB + e⁻ → A + B⁻ has threshold ΔfH°₀(A) + ΔfH°₀(B⁻) − ΔfH°₀(AB) = D₀(A–B) − EA(B); ion pairs A⁺ + B⁻ have D₀ + IE(A) − EA(B), for photons and for electron impact alike.
        • Energy diagram. Product levels are the 0 K reaction enthalpies from the reactants; your levels are drawn at the energies you enter, converted from their unit.

        Uncertainty

        The ± values are 95 % limits. ATcT values are correlated because they come from one network: when a bond energy was itself measured (H–OH, H–HCO), the parent and the fragment move together and the true uncertainty of the difference is much smaller than the independent-error estimate √Σ(νiUi)². When every species of a reaction is in ATcT's covariance file (1874 gas-phase species), the tool uses √(νᵀCν) with ATcT's covariance matrix C from TN 1.220 (the version the ATcT API serves, covariance of ΔfH° at 298.15 K). For a species whose printed uncertainty changed in TN 1.228, its row and column are scaled by U1.228/U1.220, so that its own uncertainty is the one shown on this page while its correlations stay those of TN 1.220. For D₀(H–HCO), where both H₂CO and HCO changed, that gives ±0.0066 kJ/mol instead of ±0.110 (±0.0080 with the TN 1.220 covariances as served). If the scaling would leave less than 0.1 % of the sum of the terms (strong cancellation, as in X₂ → 2 X), the value as served is used; a result that cancels below the precision of the stored correlations is shown as a bound (“< …”), never as 0. Otherwise the tool falls back to independent errors and says so. The Reaction tab shows all three numbers and which one is used; species whose printed uncertainty changed by more than ×1.5 between versions are flagged.

        Worked example

        The default reaction, CH2O -> H + HCO in kJ/mol, uses the TN 1.228 values ΔfH°₀ = 216.034 (H), 41.384 ± 0.078 (HCO) and −105.386 ± 0.078 (H₂CO) kJ/mol [3].

        1. D₀ = 216.034 + 41.384 − (−105.386) = 362.804 kJ/mol.
        2. In other units: 362.804 / 96.485 332 = 3.7602 eV, and 362.804 / 0.011 962 657 = 30 328.05 cm⁻¹.
        3. Threshold: λ = 119 626.57 / 362.804 = 329.73 nm.
        4. Independent-error uncertainty: √(0.0005² + 0.078² + 0.078²) = ±0.110 kJ/mol (H is printed as ± 0.000, taken as 0.0005). With the ATcT correlations the uncertainty is ±0.0066 kJ/mol, because H₂CO and HCO are tied by the measured bond energy.
        5. At 298.15 K: 217.998 + 41.758 − (−109.226) = 368.982 kJ/mol.

        The Reaction tab shows the same numbers, and the Self-test tab checks them against Terentis and Kable's measured 30 328.5 ± 0.5 cm⁻¹, which ATcT uses as an input.

        Assumptions and limits

        • Only species in ATcT TN 1.228 (3455 entries, about 2700 in the gas phase). Products the network does not contain are not found.
        • One uncertainty per species as printed in the ATcT table, applied at 0 K and 298.15 K. ATcT prints each value to the decimals of its uncertainty (at most 0.001 kJ/mol), so every printed value also carries up to half a last digit of rounding. Both the independent and the correlated uncertainties include that rounding (½·10−d per species, combined in quadrature); it is what limits differences where the covariances cancel, such as Br2 → 2 Br. For independent errors "± 0.000" is taken as 0.0005.
        • Thresholds are thermochemical: no barriers, no tunnelling, no spin or symmetry restrictions, no hot bands.
        • Channel search uses gas-phase entries in their listed ground states unless you include ATcT's other states and conformers (for example O(g, singlet) = O(¹D) and CH₂(g, singlet) = ¹CH₂). Ortho and para nuclear-spin entries are left out.
        • The covariances are from TN 1.220, the version the ATcT API served on 2026-11-06, while the values are TN 1.228; 2352 species changed value or uncertainty between the two. They are for 298.15 K and are applied to 0 K results as well. Pairs too weak to matter were dropped, so a variance can come out slightly negative; it is then shown as an upper bound, not as 0.
        • Excited states cover the low-lying states of 30 atoms and small molecules only. Ion fragments are always in their ground state. Huber and Herzberg's diatomic data stop in 1977. Where their reference level is not the lowest level and could not be converted (multiplet centres, hypothetical J = 0 levels), the offset, up to 146 cm⁻¹, is carried as an allowance in the uncertainty rather than corrected. Spin, symmetry and Franck–Condon factors decide whether a channel is actually reached; thresholds say only that it is energetically open.
        • The energy diagram draws your intermediates and transition states as entered; it does not check them.
        • A plain formula such as C2H4 is matched by composition and charge. When several isomers share it, the tool takes the gas-phase entry ATcT lists first (here ethylene, not ethylidene) and offers the others; check the species name in the result.
