About

Lasers, vacuum, and beams, from the lab to production

I'm Casey Foley, a laser and high vacuum systems expert with a PhD in physical chemistry. In research I built a plasma-discharge ion and fast-atom beam machine, restored a 5K cryogenic ion-trap spectrometer, and ran molecular-beam velocity-map imaging and MeV electron diffraction experiments; in production I am the subject-matter expert for all laser and high vacuum processes and develop the measurement systems that keep them in control.

Laser systems & processes

High-power fiber, DPSS, CO₂, and excimer lasers for drilling, ablation, scribing, and sealing in production; tunable dye, IR OPO/OPA, femtosecond Ti:sapphire, and 118 nm VUV sources in the lab. I set process windows with DOE and hold them with in-line sensing and SPC.

High vacuum & UHV

High vacuum ion beam lines, UHV molecular-beam chambers, a 5K cryogenic ion trap restored to full operation, and production high vacuum evacuation processes. I've taken instruments from bare chamber to publishable data, designing and ordering the hardware and maintaining the lasers and vacuum systems myself.

Ion, molecular & electron beams

A pulsed plasma-discharge ion source (N⁺, N₂⁺, O⁺, O₂⁺, Ar⁺), kilovolt Einzel and deceleration optics, quadrupole energy filtering, a charge-exchange cell, molecular beams, MCP/phosphor and velocity-map ion imaging, and MeV ultrafast electron diffraction experiments.

Spectroscopy & mass spectrometry

VUV, UV, and IR laser spectroscopy, IR–UV double resonance in a cryogenic ion trap, REMPI and LIF, and ionization and ion-mobility mass spectrometry on Waters, Thermo, and Bruker platforms. I write instrument control and analysis in Python, LabVIEW, and MATLAB.

Optical metrology & vision

Laser-based R-value measurement, phase-shifting deflectometry, confocal profilometry, laser triangulation and structured light, thermal and distortion scanning, line-scan defect inspection, and color measurement (CIELAB). I take measurement systems from algorithm to plant floor and tie them to yield data.

Process & quality engineering

FAT/SAT and commissioning of laser and vision systems, capability studies with control and spec limits, root-cause analytics, and Class 4 laser safety (ANSI Z136, FDA 21 CFR 1040). I am the technical liaison between production, R&D, and scaling.

Ion beams & plasma

Plasma-discharge ion and fast-atom beam machine

Challenge
Studying associative-ionization reactions relevant to hypersonic flight needed fast-atom and ion beams the lab did not have.
Approach
Over two years I designed and built a fast-atom/ion beam machine, from new and repurposed hardware, around a pulsed plasma-discharge source, and characterized the discharge plasmas with MCP/phosphor imaging, cameras, and PMTs. Its kilovolt beam line adds Einzel and deceleration optics, a quadrupole energy filter, and a charge-exchange cell toward electron–ion coincidence imaging; I modeled it in SIMION with custom Lua control and designed parts in SolidWorks. I also built a 118 nm VUV source by tripling 355 nm light in Xe/Ar and ran MeV ultrafast electron diffraction experiments at SLAC.
Outcome
The beam line produced N⁺, N₂⁺, O⁺, O₂⁺, and Ar⁺ beams for the program. As senior postdoc I mentored students and led lab operations, and the SLAC electron diffraction experiments produced a 2026 preprint.

Cryogenic ion-trap spectrometer recovery

Challenge
A relocated cryogenic ion-trap tandem mass spectrometer was out of service.
Approach
I restored its high vacuum and 5K cold-trap systems, aligned and calibrated a tunable IR OPO/OPA (checked against methane photoacoustic spectra) and UV dye lasers, and rebuilt its synchronized timing and scan control with arbitrary waveform generators and LabVIEW.
Outcome
I brought the instrument back to full operation, then developed IR, UV, and double-resonance methods that produced first-author papers in JACS (2024) and J. Phys. Chem. A (2023), and presented the work at the International Symposium on Molecular Spectroscopy (ISMS) in 2022.

Laser drilling and ablation in volume production

Challenge
Two production laser processes, a drilling step and an ablation step, needed higher yield and shorter cycle times.
Approach
On the drilling process I traced defects to focus height, debris, and process discipline, added in-line confocal distance sensing with auto-focus correction, and integrated a fiber laser through factory acceptance testing and commissioning. On the ablation process I added in-line depth metrology, closed-loop focus correction, vision-based part location, and SPC, and set its depth specification from first principles. A root-cause study of production data then traced a cracking defect, and I developed the laser recipe that eliminated it.
Outcome
Cracking was eliminated and other drilling defects fell from about 1 in 10 parts to 1% or less, with a 3× faster laser cycle. Ablation throughput doubled at ~99% yield.

High vacuum evacuation cycle time

Science 2021

Quantum resonances in formaldehyde roaming

Challenge
The question was whether quantum resonances couple the roaming, radical, and molecular dissociation channels of formaldehyde.
Approach
I used high-resolution velocity-map imaging with automated, tunable UV laser systems and custom optical detection on a UHV molecular-beam apparatus.
Outcome
We observed orbiting resonances that couple the three channels. I was first author on the Science paper (2021), which has 40+ citations.

Thermal-performance and flatness metrology

Challenge
Production needed reliable measurements of thermal performance and flatness to screen product and guide process changes.
Approach
I developed a proprietary laser-based thermal-performance measurement, from the algorithm through plant-floor integration, and the protocol for using it in quality screening. For flatness I led development of a phase-shifting deflectometry system (~10 µm) for in-line 100% inspection and wrote a Python pipeline for micron-level confocal profiling. Outcome
Thermal-performance measurement runs on the plant floor for quality screening, and flatness is measured directly at two scales.

In-line inspection and process capability

Challenge
Production needed dimensional and defect inspection at the line, with data the plant could act on.
Approach
I wrote the specification for an in-line line-scan system that measures dimensions and detects defects, ran a competitive vendor selection, and led factory and site acceptance testing. I set golden-part and GR&R acceptance criteria, defined its data requirements with the controls and MES teams, and held the vendor to spec with a tool-versus-manual correlation study. I then ran a capability study of the in-line QC characteristics, set their control limits, and revised their spec limits.
Outcome
The inspection system runs in production. A sensor upgrade cut one key variation by about 70%, and a capability dashboard, automated reporting, and a root-cause platform keep quality data visible across the company.
