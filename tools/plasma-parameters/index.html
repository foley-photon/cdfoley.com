Plasma Parameters Calculator

The handful of numbers that decide how a plasma behaves: how far a charge’s field reaches before the plasma screens it, how fast the electrons and ions oscillate, how often they collide, and how tightly a magnetic field holds them. Enter a density and temperature, the field and gas pressure if you have them, and the calculator also gives the electron cyclotron resonance (ECR) field for a microwave source and flags which regime the plasma is in.

Key terms
Electron density
Free electrons per cubic centimeter. Glow and RF processing discharges hold 10⁹–10¹² cm⁻³; the solar wind about 5 cm⁻³; a fusion plasma 10¹⁴ cm⁻³.
Electron temperature
The spread of electron energies, quoted as kT in electronvolts: 1 eV corresponds to 11 605 K. In a low-pressure discharge the electrons are at 1–10 eV while the gas and the ions are far colder: 0.026–0.1 eV (300–1200 K) in most discharges, with ions at a few tenths of an eV in ECR reactors.
Debye length
The distance over which the plasma screens out the electric field of a charge or a biased surface. Probes, sheaths at walls, and anything smaller than a few Debye lengths behave differently from the plasma bulk.
Plasma frequency
How fast the electrons oscillate if they are pushed away from the ions and let go. Electromagnetic waves below this frequency cannot travel through the plasma; they are reflected.
Plasma parameter
The number of electrons inside a sphere one Debye length in radius. When it is large, collective behavior dominates over close two-body encounters, which is what makes an ionized gas a plasma.
Collision frequency and mean free path
How often an electron is deflected by an ion or a gas atom, and how far it travels between such events on average.
Gyrofrequency
How often an electron or ion circles a magnetic field line: 28.0 GHz per tesla for electrons. If particles complete many turns between collisions, the field confines them.
Electron cyclotron resonance (ECR)
Where the electron gyrofrequency equals the frequency of applied microwaves, the electrons absorb energy efficiently. ECR ion sources and plasma reactors are built around this field: 0.0875 T for 2.45 GHz, 1.0 T for 28 GHz.

Regime notes

    How it is calculated

    All quantities are in SI units with temperatures as energies (\(T\) in joules is \(e\) times the value in eV). With the ion density \(n_i = n_e/Z\) (quasi-neutrality):

    \[ \begin{gathered} \lambda_{De} = \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0 T_e}{n_e e^2}}, \qquad \frac{1}{\lambda_D^2} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{De}^2} + \frac{1}{\lambda_{Di}^2}, \quad \lambda_{Di} = \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0 T_i}{n_i Z^2 e^2}}, \\[8pt] \omega_{pe} = 2\pi f_{pe} = \sqrt{\frac{n_e e^2}{\varepsilon_0 m_e}}, \qquad \omega_{pi} = \sqrt{\frac{n_i Z^2 e^2}{\varepsilon_0 m_i}}, \qquad N_D = \frac{4\pi}{3} n_e \lambda_{De}^3. \end{gathered} \]

    Some texts call \(\Lambda = 4\pi n_e \lambda_{De}^3 = 3N_D\) the plasma parameter; either way it must be much larger than 1 for the plasma to be weakly coupled. In practical units \(f_{pe} \approx 8.98\ \text{kHz} \times \sqrt{n_e/\text{cm}^{-3}}\) and \(\lambda_{De} \approx 7.43\ \text{m} \times \sqrt{(T_e/\text{eV})/(n_e/\text{cm}^{-3})}\).

    Collisions

    Coulomb collisions are counted with the Coulomb logarithm. The calculator uses the NRL Plasma Formulary’s expressions, with \(n_e\) in cm⁻³ and \(T\) in eV:

    \[ \begin{gathered} \ln\Lambda_{ei} = 23 - \ln\!\left(n_e^{1/2} Z\, T_e^{-3/2}\right) \ \ (T_e < 10Z^2\ \text{eV}), \\[6pt] \ln\Lambda_{ei} = 24 - \ln\!\left(n_e^{1/2}\, T_e^{-1}\right) \ \ (T_e > 10 Z^2\ \text{eV}), \\[8pt] \ln\Lambda_{ii} = 23 - \ln\!\left[\frac{Z^2}{T_i}\left(\frac{2 n_i Z^2}{T_i}\right)^{1/2}\right]. \end{gathered} \]

    The electron–ion and ion–ion collision frequencies are the inverse Braginskii collision times,

    \[ \nu_{ei} = \frac{4\sqrt{2\pi}\, n_e Z e^4 \ln\Lambda_{ei}}{3\,(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^2\, m_e^{1/2}\, T_e^{3/2}}, \qquad \nu_{ii} = \frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\, n_i Z^4 e^4 \ln\Lambda_{ii}}{3\,(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^2\, m_i^{1/2}\, T_i^{3/2}}, \]

    which for Z = 1 are the Formulary’s \(\nu_e = 2.91\times10^{-6}\, n_e \ln\Lambda\, T_e^{-3/2}\) s⁻¹ and \(\nu_i = 4.80\times10^{-8}\, \mu^{-1/2} n_i \ln\Lambda\, T_i^{-3/2}\) s⁻¹ (\(\mu = m_i/m_p\)). The electron–ion mean free path is \(v_{te}/\nu_{ei}\) with \(v_{te} = \sqrt{T_e/m_e}\). Electron–neutral collisions use a constant momentum-transfer cross section \(\sigma_m\) (your input) and the mean electron speed:

    \[ n_n = \frac{p}{k_B T_g}, \qquad \nu_{en} = n_n \sigma_m \bar v_e, \quad \bar v_e = \sqrt{\frac{8 T_e}{\pi m_e}}, \qquad \lambda_{en} = \frac{1}{n_n \sigma_m}. \]

    Magnetic field and microwaves

    \[ \begin{gathered} \omega_{ce} = \frac{eB}{m_e}, \qquad \omega_{ci} = \frac{ZeB}{m_i}, \qquad r_e = \frac{v_{te}}{\omega_{ce}}, \qquad r_i = \frac{\sqrt{T_i/m_i}}{\omega_{ci}}, \qquad \beta = \frac{2\mu_0 (n_e T_e + n_i T_i)}{B^2}, \\[8pt] B_\text{ECR} = \frac{2\pi f\, m_e}{e}, \qquad n_c = \frac{\varepsilon_0 m_e (2\pi f)^2}{e^2}. \end{gathered} \]

    \(B_\text{ECR}\) is the field where the electron gyrofrequency equals the microwave frequency: \(f/(27.992\ \text{GHz})\) teslas, so 2.45 GHz resonates at 87.5 mT and 28 GHz at 1.000 T. Above the cutoff density \(n_c\) (where \(f_{pe} = f\)) an ordinary wave cannot propagate into the plasma: 7.4 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³ at 2.45 GHz and 9.7 × 10¹² cm⁻³ at 28 GHz. ECR sources reach higher densities with waves that travel along the field from the high-field side or by mode conversion.

    Worked example

    The default inputs: an argon plasma with ne = 10¹¹ cm⁻³, Te = 5 eV, Ti = 0.05 eV, in 1 mTorr of gas at 20 °C, at the 87.5 mT resonance of a 2.45 GHz source, in a 10 cm chamber.

    • \(\lambda_{De}^2 = 8.854\times10^{-12} \times 5 / (10^{17} \times 1.602\times10^{-19}) = 2.763\times10^{-9}\ \text{m}^2\), so \(\lambda_{De} = 52.6\ \mu\text{m}\) and ND = 6.1 × 10⁴: a weakly coupled plasma, and the 10 cm chamber is about 1900 Debye lengths across.
    • fpe = 8.98 kHz × √10¹¹ = 2.84 GHz, above the 2.45 GHz drive: the plasma is over-dense (ne = 1.34 nc), so an ordinary wave from outside is cut off.
    • ln Λ = 23 − ln(√10¹¹ × 5⁻¹·⁵) = 12.75, and νei = 2.91 × 10⁻⁶ × 10¹¹ × 12.75 / 5¹·⁵ = 3.3 × 10⁵ s⁻¹. The gas (nn = 3.3 × 10¹³ cm⁻³, so the plasma is 0.3 % ionized) gives νen = 4.9 × 10⁶ s⁻¹ and an electron–neutral mean free path of 30 cm, longer than the chamber.
    • At B = 87.5 mT, fce = 2.45 GHz, so ωce/νe ≈ 2900 (about 470 turns between collisions) with a thermal gyroradius of 61 µm: they are strongly magnetized. Argon ions at Ti = 0.05 eV (fci = 33.6 kHz, ri = 1.6 mm) collide with each other more often than they gyrate (ωci/νii = 0.56), and β = 2.7 × 10⁻⁵, so the field is far stronger than the plasma pressure. Above Ti ≈ 0.08 eV the ions gyrate faster than they collide with each other; at the 0.25–0.5 eV measured across the field in argon ECR reactors, ri = 3.7–5.2 mm and ωci/νii = 4.4–11.

    Assumptions and limits

    • Maxwellian electrons and ions, each at one temperature. Discharge electrons often have a non-Maxwellian tail, which changes the electron–neutral rate a great deal and the Coulomb rates a little.
    • The Coulomb logarithm formulas need ln Λ well above 2 (weak coupling); the calculator warns when ND is below about 10. Factors of order unity in the definitions of collision frequency and mean free path differ between texts: the electron–ion rate here is Braginskii’s, the rate of momentum loss, not the rate of 90° deflections.
    • The electron–neutral rate uses one energy-independent cross section; ion–neutral collisions (charge exchange dominates for ions in their parent gas) are not calculated, so the ion magnetization note considers ion–ion collisions only.
    • Single ion species and quasi-neutrality. Near walls (in the sheath, a few Debye lengths thick) ne ≠ Z ni.
    • The ECR field and cutoff density are for cold electrons and an ordinary wave; relativistic and hot-plasma corrections shift the resonance at high electron energies (by the factor γ for electrons of tens of keV in ECR ion sources).
