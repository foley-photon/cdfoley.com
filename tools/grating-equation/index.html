How it is calculated

For a plane reflection grating with groove spacing \(d\), light incident at angle \(\alpha\) is diffracted into angles \(\beta\) that satisfy the grating equation

\[ m\lambda = d\,(\sin\alpha + \sin\beta) . \]

Sign convention: both angles are measured from the grating normal and are positive on the same side of it. The zero order is the specular reflection, \(\beta = -\alpha\), and orders \(\pm m\) fall on opposite sides of it. An order exists only if \(|\sin\beta| \le 1\). Otherwise it is evanescent and carries no power.

Mountings

  • Littrow: the light returns along its incoming path, \(\alpha = \beta\), so \(\sin\alpha = m\lambda/2d\).
  • Constant deviation (Czerny–Turner): the angle between the incident and diffracted beams, \(D_V = \alpha - \beta\), is fixed by the instrument, and the grating rotates to scan. With scan angle \(\phi = (\alpha+\beta)/2\), the equation becomes \(m\lambda = 2d\cos(D_V/2)\sin\phi\), so \(\alpha = \phi + D_V/2\) and \(\beta = \phi - D_V/2\).

Dispersion, resolution, and bandpass

\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{d\beta}{d\lambda} = \frac{m}{d\cos\beta}, \\[4pt] \frac{d\lambda}{dx} = \frac{d\cos\beta}{m\,L_B}, \\[4pt] R = \frac{\lambda}{\Delta\lambda} = |m|N = \frac{W\,|\sin\alpha + \sin\beta|}{\lambda} . \end{gathered} \]

The reciprocal linear dispersion \(d\lambda/dx\) (nm per mm at the focal plane) sets the bandpass: \(\text{BP} \approx w\cdot d\lambda/dx\) for a slit or pixel of width \(w\). This assumes the imaged entrance-slit width matches the exit slit or pixel. The resolving power \(R = |m|N\) is the theoretical limit set by the number of illuminated grooves. Practical spectrometers are usually slit- or pixel-limited well before reaching it. The results give the bandpass and the diffraction-limited width \(\lambda/R\) in wavelength, frequency, and wavenumber: \(\Delta\nu = c\,\Delta\lambda/\lambda^2\) and \(\Delta\tilde\nu = \Delta\lambda/\lambda^2\). The free spectral range \(\lambda/|m|\) is the band before the next order overlaps, so order-sorting filters are needed when \(\lambda_\text{max} > 2\lambda_\text{min}\) in first order.

Worked example

Take a 1200 lines/mm grating (\(d = 833.3\) nm) at \(\alpha = 30^\circ\), first order, 500 nm. Then \(\sin\beta = 0.6 - 0.5 = 0.1\), so \(\beta = 5.74^\circ\). The angular dispersion is 1.206 mrad/nm. With a 300 mm focusing mirror the reciprocal linear dispersion is 2.76 nm/mm, so a 50 µm slit passes about 0.14 nm (5.5 cm⁻¹). Fifty millimeters of illuminated grating gives \(R = 60\,000\), or 8.3 pm (10 GHz) at 500 nm. In a Czerny–Turner with \(D_V = 30^\circ\), the same wavelength needs \(\alpha = 33.09^\circ\) and \(\beta = 3.09^\circ.\)

Assumptions and limits

  • Plane grating in the principal plane. No conical (off-plane) diffraction. Concave and holographic aberration-corrected gratings follow the same equation at their center, but their focal-plane properties differ.
  • Geometric bandpass. BP ≈ w·dλ/dx ignores aberrations, diffraction from the optics, and the anamorphic magnification \(\cos\alpha/\cos\beta\). That magnification makes the entrance-slit image differ in width from the slit itself when \(\alpha \ne \beta\).
  • Detector normal to the diffracted beam. \(d\lambda/dx\) is for a focal plane perpendicular to the central diffracted ray. A detector tilted by \(\gamma\) from that plane spreads the spectrum by \(1/\cos\gamma\), so \(d\lambda/dx = d\cos\beta\cos\gamma/(m\,L_B)\).
  • Efficiency is not modeled. Which orders are bright depends on blaze angle, groove profile, polarization, and coating. Use the manufacturer's efficiency curves. A blazed grating is brightest where its groove facets reflect the incident beam into the diffracted one, \(\alpha + \beta = 2\theta_B\). Catalogs quote the blaze wavelength for Littrow, \(m\lambda_B = 2d\sin\theta_B\), so a grating blazed at the Littrow angle in the results peaks at \(\lambda\) when used in Littrow. In a constant-deviation mount the peak moves to \(\lambda_B\cos(D_V/2)\).
