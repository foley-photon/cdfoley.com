Focused Laser Spot Size Calculator

How small a spot a lens focuses a laser beam to, and how long the focus stays that small. Use it to choose a focal length and beam size for a target spot, or to check what a given laser, lens, and scanner will deliver. It accounts for beam quality (M²) and for clipping by the lens or scanner aperture, which it calculates from diffraction theory rather than a lookup table, so it stays accurate when the beam nearly fills the aperture.

Key terms
Spot diameter (1/e²)
The diameter of the focused beam where the intensity has fallen to 13.5 % of its peak. An unclipped Gaussian spot holds 86.5 % of the power inside it.
Focal length
The effective focal length f′ of the focusing or f-theta lens, from its datasheet. It is not the working distance to the part.
Input beam diameter
The 1/e² diameter of the beam arriving at the lens, after any beam expander.
Clear aperture
The usable diameter of the smallest opening the beam passes: the lens, a scanner mirror, or an iris.
Truncation ratio
Beam diameter divided by aperture diameter, T. Below about 0.4 the aperture has almost no effect; at 0.5 it makes the spot 1.7 % larger. At 1 the aperture cuts the beam at its 1/e² edge and passes 86.5 % of the power.
APO (apodization) factor
The number that sets how large the focused spot is for a given beam and lens: 4/π ≈ 1.27 for an unclipped Gaussian beam, and larger when the aperture clips the beam.
M² (beam quality)
How many times larger the focused spot is than that of an ideal Gaussian beam of the same size through the same lens. 1 is perfect.
Depth of focus
The length along the beam, centered on the focus, over which the spot radius stays within your tolerance. It shrinks with the square of the spot size.

Vacuum wavelength from the laser datasheet. In air it is 0.03 % shorter, which can be ignored here.

How it is calculated

For a beam with 1/e² diameter \(D_b\) at a lens of focal length \(f'\), the focused 1/e² spot diameter is

\[ d_F = \mathrm{APO}\cdot\frac{\lambda\, f'\, M^2}{D_b} \]

The apodization factor APO depends on the truncation ratio \(T = D_b/D_t\), where \(D_t\) is the smallest clear aperture the beam passes through (for \(M^2 > 1\), on \(T/M\); see below). For an untruncated Gaussian (\(T \lesssim 0.4\)), \(\mathrm{APO} = 4/\pi \approx 1.273\) and the formula reduces to the textbook result \(d_F = 4\lambda f' M^2/(\pi D_b)\), or \(w_0 = M^2\lambda f'/(\pi w_L)\) in radius form, with \(w_L = D_b/2\).

Truncation and the APO factor

A larger input beam gives a smaller spot, but only until the aperture starts to clip it. Clipping diffracts light out of the central lobe. The focal profile then moves away from a Gaussian toward an Airy pattern, and the spot stops shrinking in proportion to \(1/D_b\). This calculator does not use a lookup table. It evaluates the Fraunhofer diffraction integral of a truncated Gaussian,

\[ U(r) \propto \int_0^{D_t/2} e^{-\rho^2/w_L^2}\, J_0\!\left(\frac{2\pi \rho r}{\lambda f'}\right)\rho\, d\rho , \]

and finds the radius at which \(|U|^2\) falls to \(e^{-2}\) of its peak. The table gives the result for a TEM₀₀ beam (\(M^2 = 1\)). The widely quoted curve fit in the CVI Melles Griot guide, of the kind Urey derived from the same integral, writes the spot as \(d_F = K\lambda f'/D_t\), so \(K = \mathrm{APO}/T\). It agrees with the integral to within 0.7 % for \(T \ge 0.8\) but runs 1–3 % low for \(T\) = 0.5–0.7.

Where to stop is a trade-off between spot size and lost power. For a fixed aperture, going from T = 0.7 to 1 shrinks the spot by only 11 % while the clipped power rises from 1.7 % to 13.5 %, and going on to T = 2 gains about 8 % more at a 60 % loss. Optics guides give T ≈ 0.7–1.0 as the usual range for focusing lenses (CVI Melles Griot). Scan heads are held to a stricter limit: clipped power heats the mirrors and housing, so the aperture should be at least 1.52 times the beam diameter (T ≤ 0.66, about 1 % clipped), with extra margin for beam pointing and alignment errors (Novanta).

A multimode beam (\(M^2 > 1\)) is usually only partially coherent across the aperture, so clipping broadens its focus less than it would a TEM₀₀ beam of the same diameter. The calculator therefore evaluates APO at \(T/M\), with \(M = \sqrt{M^2}\): the truncation ratio of the embedded TEM₀₀ beam of diameter \(D_b/M\). Compared with an exact calculation for a partially coherent beam (see the limits below), taking APO at \(T\) instead would overstate the spot at \(T = 1\) by 5.5 %, 14 %, and 40 % for \(M^2 = 1.1\), 1.3, and 5.

Depth of focus

The Rayleigh range \(z_R = \pi w_0^2/(M^2\lambda)\) is the distance over which the beam radius grows by \(\sqrt{2}\) (the area doubles and the peak fluence halves). A process that depends on fluence often tolerates much less than that. For a maximum allowed spot growth \(\varepsilon\) (e.g. 5 %), the usable depth of focus is

\[ \mathrm{DOF} = 2\,z_R\sqrt{(1+\varepsilon)^2 - 1}. \]

This is the total range, half on each side of the waist. Because \(z_R \propto w_0^2\), halving the spot size cuts the depth of focus to a quarter. Tight-focus ablation and scribing processes therefore often need height sensing or tracking optics.

Worked example

A fiber laser at 1064 nm with M² = 1.1 and an 8 mm beam, focused by a 100 mm lens with a 12 mm clear aperture: T = 0.667 and T/M = 0.636, so APO = 1.383 and

\[ d_F = 1.383 \times \frac{1.064\,\mu\text{m}\times 100\,\text{mm}\times 1.1}{8\,\text{mm}} \approx 20.2\,\mu\text{m}. \]

That gives \(z_R \approx 0.27\,\text{mm}\), and with a 5 % growth tolerance the depth of focus is only about 0.18 mm in total (±0.09 mm about the waist). Without the aperture the spot would be 18.6 µm. The 12 mm aperture costs about 9 % in spot size and 1.1 % in power.

Assumptions and limits

  • Paraxial, aberration-free lens. Real lenses add spherical aberration. A simple spherical singlet stays diffraction-limited only at modest apertures, about f/7 or slower in Edmund Optics’ comparison, and slower still at long focal lengths and short wavelengths: at a fixed f-number the aberration grows in proportion to the focal length, while the diffraction-limited spot does not. Aspheres, corrected doublets, and f-theta objectives do far better. Use the lens vendor’s spot data or a ray trace for fast lenses or when θ is large.
  • Collimated input with the waist near the lens. The focus then lies close to \(f'\), provided \(f'\) is much shorter than the input beam’s Rayleigh range \(z_{R,\mathrm{in}} = \pi D_b^2/(4M^2\lambda)\). Otherwise \(d_F\) is the spot size at \(f'\), but the true waist is smaller by \(1/\sqrt{1+(f'/z_{R,\mathrm{in}})^2}\) and forms closer to the lens, at \(f'/[1+(f'/z_{R,\mathrm{in}})^2]\) (Self’s lens equation with the input waist at the lens); the calculator flags this when the difference exceeds 1 %. A diverging or converging input shifts the focus. See the Gaussian beam propagation tool.
  • M² enters through the embedded Gaussian. The spot is \(M\) times that of the embedded TEM₀₀ beam (diameter \(D_b/M\)) clipped by the same aperture. This is exact for untruncated beams. With clipping, it agrees with an exact calculation for a partially coherent (Gaussian Schell-model) beam to within about 4 % for \(T \le 1\). For \(T \le 2\) it errs on the large side by up to about 26 %, and on the small side by at most 2 %. When the aperture is much smaller than the beam, any beam gives the Airy spot \(1.645\,\lambda f'/D_t\), and the rule overstates the spot by up to a factor \(M\).
  • Truncated beams are no longer Gaussian. When T > ~0.8, \(z_R\) and DOF from the 1/e² radius are approximate, and side lobes outside the first dark ring carry a growing share of the transmitted power (for M² = 1: about 1.7 % at T = 0.8, 4.5 % at T = 1, 12 % at T = 2, and 16 % for uniform illumination).
  • 1/e² is not D4σ for a clipped beam. The spot here is the 1/e² diameter. The second-moment (D4σ) diameter that ISO 11146-1 uses is formally infinite for the focus of a hard-edged aperture, because the side lobes fall off only as \(1/r^3\). A camera’s D4σ reading of a clipped focus therefore depends on its integration window and background subtraction.
